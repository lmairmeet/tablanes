TabLanes turns the emptiest page in your browser into the most useful one.

Every day you open dozens of new tabs. Each one greets you with a search box and
a grid of sites you already know how to reach. That space is some of the most
valuable real estate you own — you look at it more often than you look at almost
any app on your machine — and it is doing nothing for you.

TabLanes puts your board there. Open a tab, and your work is already on screen:
lanes, cards, what is in progress, what is waiting. No app to switch to, no tab
to hunt for, no login, no loading spinner. Just the board, instantly, every time.


A BOARD, NOT AN APP

TabLanes is deliberately one board. Not a workspace, not a set of projects, not
a hierarchy of teams and boards and sub-boards you have to navigate before you
can see anything. One board, on your new tab, for the work in front of you.

That single decision is what makes it fast. There is nothing to choose when the
page opens, because there is only one thing it can show you. The board is on
screen before you have finished the keystroke that opened the tab.


LANES

Create as many lanes as your work needs. The classic three — To do, Doing, Done
— are there when you first open it, and you can rename them to anything: Today
and This Week and Someday; Inbox and Triaged and Shipped; one lane per client;
one lane per repository; one lane per child's school week. Click a lane title to
rename it in place. Drag a lane by its header to move it. Each lane shows a live
count of the cards inside it.

Lanes scroll horizontally, so a wide board is no problem, and each lane scrolls
independently, so a deep lane never pushes the rest of the board around.


CARDS

Adding a card takes one click and one line of text. The composer stays open
after you add, so you can empty your head into a lane in a single burst — type,
Enter, type, Enter — instead of clicking "add" over and over. Escape closes it.

Open a card and you get room to think:

  • A title you can rewrite at any time
  • A description that grows as you type, for the context that will not fit in a
    title — the reason this matters, what "done" means, what you tried already
  • Links, each saved with an optional label and shown with its source, so a
    card can point at the pull request, the design file, the ticket and the
    thread that produced it
  • Files and images, dropped straight onto the card or chosen from a dialog

Every change saves itself as you type. There is no save button, because there is
nothing to remember to press.


FILES AND IMAGES THAT ACTUALLY LIVE ON THE CARD

Most lightweight task tools give you a text field and expect you to keep the
real material somewhere else — a folder, a chat thread, a cloud drive. TabLanes
keeps it on the card.

Drop a screenshot onto a card and it becomes the card's cover, visible on the
board at a glance. Click it to open it full size in a lightbox. Attach a PDF, a
spreadsheet, a font file, a zip, a video — anything — and download it back out
whenever you need it. Attachments are listed with their type and size, and a
card shows small badges for how many links and files it carries, so you can see
which cards are dense and which are still just a line of text.

Because the files are stored locally, attaching a large image is instant. There
is no upload, no progress bar, and no quiet compression of your screenshots.


DRAG AND DROP THAT FEELS RIGHT

Moving a card is the single most common thing you do on a board, so it got the
most attention.

Pick up a card and it follows your cursor immediately, at a slight tilt, while
the cards around it glide out of the way to show you exactly where it will land.
Drag toward the edge of the board and it scrolls, faster the closer you get, so
crossing a wide board is one continuous motion rather than a series of drops and
re-grabs. Lanes reorder the same way, by their headers.

This is built on pointer events rather than the browser's built-in drag and
drop, which is why it tracks your cursor exactly instead of lagging behind it,
and why it behaves the same with a mouse, a trackpad, or a touchscreen.


FIND ANYTHING, IMMEDIATELY

Press the forward slash key from anywhere on the board and start typing. Lanes
filter down to matching cards as you type, matched text is highlighted inside
each card, every lane shows how many of its cards matched, and the header keeps
a running count. Search looks at titles and descriptions both, so a card you
wrote three weeks ago is findable by any detail you put in it.

Escape clears the search and returns the whole board. Dragging still works while
a filter is active, and cards land exactly where you drop them relative to the
hidden ones — a small detail that takes real care to get right, and that you
should never have to think about.


UNDO, BECAUSE MISTAKES HAPPEN

Delete a card and it goes, along with its links and its files — but a toast
appears offering it back. One click restores the card exactly as it was, with
every attachment intact.

Delete a lane and the same applies: the lane and every card inside it come back
in their original order, in their original position on the board. Nothing is
destroyed the moment you click, and nothing asks you to confirm a decision you
are entitled to reverse.


BUILT TO DISAPPEAR

TabLanes follows your system theme. Dark by default, with a light theme that
takes over automatically when your machine is in light mode. Both were designed
first rather than converted from each other.

The interface is quiet on purpose. No sidebars, no ribbons, no onboarding
checklist, no upsell banner, no badge asking you to invite your team, no bright
empty states telling you to get started. A header with a search box, your lanes,
and your cards. The board is the product.


YOUR DATA IS A DATABASE ON YOUR COMPUTER

TabLanes stores your board in SQLite — the actual database engine, compiled to
WebAssembly and bundled inside the extension. Lanes, cards, links and
attachments are real tables with real relationships: delete a lane and its cards
go with it because the database enforces it, not because some code remembered
to clean up.

That database lives inside your browser profile on your own machine. Your
attachments live beside it. Nothing is uploaded, because there is nowhere to
upload it to. TabLanes has no server.

It also has no account. You do not sign up, you do not sign in, you do not
connect anything, and you do not agree to anything. You install it, and your
board is there.


NO NETWORK. NONE.

TabLanes makes no network requests of any kind. Not for analytics, not for
telemetry, not for crash reports, not for fonts, not for favicons, not for an
update check, not once.

There is no remote code: every file the extension runs ships inside the package
you install, which is why the whole thing works with the network off, on a
plane, or behind the strictest firewall your employer operates. The board that
loads when you are offline is the same board, complete, with every file you have
ever attached to it.

It asks for a single permission — unlimitedStorage — so that a browser quota
never quietly truncates your board when you attach large files. It cannot read
your browsing history, your open tabs, or the contents of any page you visit,
because it never asks for the ability to.

Removing the extension deletes the database and the attachments with it. There
is no copy anywhere else, and no account to close.


FAST, MEASURED HONESTLY

Speed here is not a slogan. The board is a local database query and a render:
there is no request in the critical path, so there is nothing to be slow.
Reordering a card is a single database update rather than a renumbering of every
card in the lane, which is why dragging stays smooth in a lane with hundreds of
cards. Writes are batched and flushed the moment the tab goes away, so closing a
tab a half-second after you type never costs you the thing you typed.


WHAT IT DOES NOT DO

Being clear about this saves you a download.

TabLanes does not sync between machines, because it has no server to sync
through. It has no collaborators, no comments, no assignees, no notifications.
It has no due dates, labels, checklists, or automations. It is one board, not
many. If you need a shared project tracker for a team, you want a different
product, and you already know which ones.

What it does is hold your own work, in front of you, instantly, privately, every
time you open a tab.


WHO IT IS FOR

People who keep a task list and resent how much friction stands between them and
it. Developers who want the current branch's work visible without leaving the
browser. Writers tracking drafts through stages. Researchers with a lane of
papers to read and a lane of papers read. Students with a lane per course. Job
hunters with a lane per stage of the pipeline and a card per company, each one
carrying the posting, the contact and the version of the CV they sent. Anyone
who has ever pinned a task tracker to a tab and then lost it behind forty other
tabs.

And anyone who would simply prefer their to-do list not to live on someone
else's computer.


SMALL THINGS YOU WILL NOTICE

Enter adds the card and keeps the composer open; Shift and Enter together give
you a second line. Escape backs out of whatever you are in — the composer, the
search box, an open card. Clicking outside a card closes it, and everything you
typed is already saved.


GETTING STARTED

Install it and open a new tab. Three lanes are waiting. Rename them to whatever
your work is actually called, add a card, and you are running. There is no
tutorial because there is nothing to learn: it is a board, and it behaves the
way you expect a board to behave.


OPEN SOURCE

TabLanes is open source. Every line it runs is public, which is the only way a
claim like "this never touches the network" can mean anything — you do not have
to take our word for it, you can read the code, or watch the network panel stay
empty for as long as you like.
